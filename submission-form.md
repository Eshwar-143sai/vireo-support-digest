# Submission form answers

**What did you build, and what business outcome does it move? State the number and the money.**
A no-API-key Python tool (`python run.py`, 12 s) that turns the ticket export into a weekly complaint digest, a fair per-team agent leaderboard and a duplicate-payout detector. Outcome it moves: orders paid out twice (refund + replacement, or refunds above order value). Today 130 of 1,833 checkable paid orders (7.1%), Rs 3.09 lakh over 17.9 months = Rs 51,770/quarter. Goal: 7.1% -> 2%, about Rs 37,000/quarter (Rs 26k at 50% prevented, Rs 47k at 90%). It is a lower bound (exact order-ID links only); the "prevented" share is an assumption, not measured.

**What does one run cost, and what would a month cost at ~650 tickets a week? Show the arithmetic. If you used no paid calls, say so.**
No paid calls in the tool: Rs 0 per run, Rs 0 a month (650/week x 4.33 = 2,815 tickets/month; classification is regex + typo repair on a laptop, ~12 s for 11,875 tickets). Finance's "no November surprise" is therefore met by construction. If a cheap model were later added only for the ~1% unclassified plus ~18% notes-only tickets (about 20% of 2,815 = ~560 tickets/month x ~300 tokens = ~170k tokens/month), that would be well under Rs 100/month at current small-model prices (illustrative; check current pricing). Note: the export averages ~153 tickets/week, not 650. AI assistant spend: Rs 0 (Claude Free Tier + Google Antigravity / Gemini).

**How do you know it works? Sample size, how you checked, error rate, and the kind of case it gets wrong.**
Classifier: three random hand audits (126, 100, 79 tickets), each drawn after the previous round of fixes. As audited: 82.5% -> 89.0% -> 89.9% theme accuracy; pooled on the two samples the rules had not seen, 89.4% (160/179), 91% at family level. Caveat: labels are a single-rater audit by an AI, not a gold standard; I accepted predictions that looked right (anchoring risk). It gets wrong: heavy-typo rare phrasings, delivery-vs-pickup ambiguity, product names acting as keywords, notes-only tickets. Payout detector: 3 unit tests on synthetic orders plus manual reading of the top cases; no ground truth exists for "should not have been paid". 9 automated tests pass; a regression test replays every audited ticket.

**Did you change, narrow, or push back on the client's ask? What, when, and why.**
Yes, the leaderboard. Priya asked for tickets closed per week per agent (and reaffirmed it on 9 Sep). Raw volume ranks teams, not effort (Billing/Logistics/Returns ~5.4/agent/week vs Chat 2.9, Voice 2.6), Tier 2 must not be compared on volume (policy s6, Neha), and breaches are structural (Night 12.9% vs Day 6.8%). I built Tier 1 ranked within team, Tier 2 by days to resolve, and kept the raw table with a warning so she still sees what she asked for. I also reframed the goal from "reading tool" to money, per Arjun's email, and used Rs 290 (policy) not Rs 180.

**What is wrong with what you are handing us? Be specific.**
(1) Single AI rater for the accuracy audit; final rules were patched after the third sample, so final accuracy is unmeasured (expected ~90%). (2) Second-payout logic is a rule, not proof of wrongdoing: exceptions such as approved warranty buy-back after a replacement will be flagged. (3) "70% preventable" is assumed. (4) Order links via customer+SKU fallback are excluded from the payout number and used elsewhere (lot watch is exact-link only). (5) Legacy +5h30 shift inferred from negative durations, not documented per row; no legacy refund unit problem was visible, none applied. (6) Repeat-contact definition uses my themes, so its error rate inherits the classifier's. (7) Digest "Signal" thresholds (>=8 tickets, 1.5x baseline, Poisson p<0.01) are judgment calls. (8) Notes-only classification (about 18%) reflects what agents did. (9) Digest quotes customer text (omitted from the public sample). (10) I did not see whether the email thread continued after the 9 Sep message. (11) Leaderboard weights (40/30/30) are arbitrary; teams of 3-7 agents make ranks noisy.

**What did you deliberately leave out, and why that rather than something else?**
Dashboard/UI (Priya said no platform); LLM classification (cost risk, rules hit ~90%); a helpdesk integration for the refund warning (the real fix, but needs IT and is outside 5 hours); CSAT modelling (45% response, weak signal); staffing model for SLA credits (Rs 62k/quarter, real but a different project); transfer-routing analysis (Rs 60k/quarter); a definitive lot-defect call (does not survive multiple-testing correction); per-agent coaching from notes. Chose the payout leak because it is the only pool where the fix is a single control, the numbers are checkable in the data and the value does not depend on the disputed contact cost.

**Anything you built or found that nobody asked for?**
The double-payout finding itself and the mechanism (92% raised by a different agent, 66% different team, median 23 days later); the finding that the bot's "Other" tag (14%) is 99% identifiable; the lot-outlier watch (Pulse 2 lot PL2-2509-4, 19 faults vs ~7 expected, not significant after correction); evidence that agent differences in CSAT/repeat are mostly chance; Diya Singh (Billing) as a genuine volume outlier at equal quality.
